# Regulatory Escalation Assessment

## Purpose

This document demonstrates how a cybersecurity analyst may identify potential regulatory considerations and escalate them to the appropriate legal, compliance, risk, and management personnel.

<sup>*It does not make a final legal or regulatory determination.</sup>

---

## Scenario Assessment

The synthetic incident confirms:

- Unauthorized access to one employee identity
- External mailbox forwarding
- Suspicious mailbox-rule modification
- Payment-related internal email activity

The current dataset does not confirm:

- Exposure of sensitive member information
- Financial loss
- Disruption of member services
- Compromise of a member information system
- Significant operational disruption

---

## NCUA Cyber Incident Notification

Federally insured credit unions are subject to NCUA cyber incident notification requirements under 12 CFR Part 748.

A federally insured credit union that reasonably believes it experienced a reportable cyber incident must notify the NCUA as soon as possible and no later than <ins>**72 hours**</ins> after forming that reasonable belief.

<sub>*The initial notification is intended to provide timely awareness and does not require completion of the full forensic investigation.</sub>

---

## Reporting Assessment

The synthetic account compromise should be escalated internally for review of potential regulatory obligations.

Based solely on the current lab evidence, a final determination that the incident meets the NCUA definition of a reportable cyber incident cannot be made.

Additional facts would be required, including:

- Whether sensitive member information was accessed or exposed
- Whether member information systems were affected
- Whether vital member services were disrupted
- Whether significant financial or operational impact occurred
- Whether the incident represents a substantial loss of confidentiality, integrity, or availability

---

## Escalation Path

Promptly notify the organization's designated incident-response leadership and involve appropriate stakeholders.

Potential stakeholders include:

- Cybersecurity leadership
- Information technology leadership
- Enterprise risk
- Compliance
- Legal counsel
- Fraud operations
- Business leadership
- Privacy personnel

<sub>*Final regulatory determinations should be made by personnel authorized by the organization.</sub>

---

## Information to Preserve

The investigation should preserve:

- Date and time the incident was identified
- Timeline of relevant activity
- Affected business functions
- Known operational impact
- Known or suspected data exposure
- Containment actions
- Current investigation status

---

## Analyst Responsibility

The analyst's responsibility is to:

1. Identify facts.
2. Preserve evidence.
3. Assess technical impact.
4. Document uncertainty.
5. Escalate potential regulatory implications promptly.
6. Support authorized personnel with accurate incident information.

<sub>*The analyst should not delay escalation while attempting to complete every aspect of the forensic investigation.</sub>
